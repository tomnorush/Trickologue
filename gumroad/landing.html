What sits under the tricks.

Four things the book teaches before and between the flips. Every trick is built twice: once in the head and once in the body.

The Art of Falling

Learn to fall before you learn to flip. Spread the impact, tuck the chin, never catch yourself on straight arms.

Basics

A messy basic becomes a ceiling. Eyes on the target, extend your toes on every kick.

Discipline

Three focused sessions a week beat one heroic one. Film every session so you can see what to fix.

Fear

Fear is not the enemy. It is information. Earn the attempt on a soft surface, then decide before you go.

Questions before you start.

What do I get when I buy?

Two things, by email, straight after checkout: the 60-page book as a PDF to download, and your link to the Trickologue app to open on your phone. You pay once and keep both.

Am I too old to start?

Age changes your timeline, not the answer. Start at Level I. A longer warm-up and daily stretching do the rest.

How long until my first backflip?

In the book's sample path, a first backflip lands on a trampoline or foam pit in weeks 9 to 12, at three sessions a week. The floor version comes after, one surface at a time. Treat the weeks as a guide, not a deadline.

Do I need a gym?

Kicks and setups need no equipment. Any flat surface works. New flips and twists follow the surface ladder: trampoline, air track or foam pit first, then mats, grass and finally the floor.

Is it dangerous?

Tricking carries a real risk of injury. Every flip in the book opens with a safety cue: soft ground, a full warm-up, a spotter. Rolls and bails come first. Never try a new flip when you are tired, and learn every double under qualified coaching.

What if I'm scared?

Fear is normal. The book names two kinds. Warning fear says you skipped a step, the surface is wrong or you are tired: go back one step. Habit fear is what is left when your drills and the soft-surface version are solid. That is the fear that shows the way.

Do I need flexibility?

Level I starts where you are. The book's daily routine takes fifteen minutes, and fifteen minutes every day does more than an hour once a week.

I'm a coach. Can I use it with my athletes?

Yes. Teach from the level boards and print the progress tracker and training log for your group. For copies for a whole club, write to Tomas on Instagram.

What language is it in?

English. Trick names stay in English, as trickers use them everywhere.

Can I train with Tomas in person?

Yes, in Vilnius. Book a training and pick a time that suits you. New flips are learned on soft ground with a spotter.
